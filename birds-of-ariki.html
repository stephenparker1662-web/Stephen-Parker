Birds of Ariki

A garden in recovery, and the native birds that have come back to it — a photographic record, in progress.

By Stephen Parker · Photo essay

Birds of Ariki is a photographic record of the native birds that have made their home in this Wellington garden — from tūī and kākā working the flax and kōwhai, to the smaller, shyer species that are easy to miss: riflemen, silvereyes, fantails and the occasional kingfisher passing through.

Once a rare sighting, tūī and kākā are now everyday visitors — a sign of a wider resurgence in native birdlife across the city's gardens and reserves.

Some visitors are bold and unmistakable. Others — the riflemen, the silvereyes — take patience, a long lens, and a lot of waiting.
